Aquaculture takes the lead

Catch weight alone cannot describe the fisheries economy. Production value provides a starting point for understanding how the industry has changed across regions and commodities.

A lead that held

Aquaculture first passed state wild catch in 2011–12. State wild catch briefly regained the lead. From 2016–17, aquaculture stayed ahead every year.

Growth has eased from its peak

Aquaculture peaked at A$2.27 billion in 2022–23. Its value fell 7.4% over the next two years. It still reached A$2.10 billion in 2024–25.

Tasmania drove the aquaculture boom

National growth can conceal very different regional experiences. Its geography reveals whether expansion was widespread or concentrated in a small part of the country.

Growth concentrated in Tasmania

By 2024–25, Tasmania accounted for 62% of Australia's aquaculture value. Its contribution shows how strongly the national industry depends on production in one state.

Western Australia moved against the trend

While six states and territories grew, Western Australia ended below its 1998–99 level. The national rise therefore masks a different local experience: aquaculture growth was widespread, but not universal.

Australia's fisheries economy varies from place to place

Regional growth and regional dependence are different questions. A place can rely heavily on aquaculture without contributing a large share of national production value.

Tasmania became an aquaculture stronghold

Aquaculture's share rose from 33% to 91% between 1998–99 and 2024–25, growing from roughly one-third to almost all of Tasmania's state-managed fisheries production value.

Western Australia became less aquaculture-dependent

Aquaculture's share fell from 32% to 19% across the same period, becoming a smaller part of Western Australia's fisheries mix. State wild catch remained the larger contributor to local production value.

FROM SHARE TO SCALE

Aquaculture dependence shows farming's importance to each jurisdiction.
Absolute production value reveals where the industry is concentrated.

Tasmania stands apart

Its aquaculture value reached A$1.29 billion, nearly six times Queensland's. Farming was the main contributor to Tasmania's combined state-managed production value.

Western Australia leads wild catch

Its state wild-catch value reached A$503 million, more than double South Australia's. The two leading states had contrasting strengths: wild catch in Western Australia and aquaculture in Tasmania.

A handful of commodities shape Australia's fisheries economy

Location explains only one dimension of the fisheries economy. Different commodities contribute different amounts of weight and value, and their importance can change over time.

Salmonids stand above the rest

Salmonids, including salmon and trout, generated A$1.27 billion in 2024-25. Their 34% share means one commodity group contributed about one-third of national production value.

Rock lobsters punch above their weight

They contributed 16.7% of production value from just 3.7% of total quantity. Together, rock lobsters and salmonids generated just over half of national production value.

Salmonids rose to the top

Salmonids climbed from seventh to first by production value, first reaching the top in 2010-11. They briefly slipped to second before returning to first, so their rise was not uninterrupted.

Rock lobsters stayed highly valuable

Rock lobsters moved from first to third between the endpoints, remaining among the three highest-value groups in 2024-25. Rank measures relative position: a lower rank does not by itself establish falling production value.

One group changed the scale

Salmonids added A$1.18 billion in production value. That represents 73% of the net commodity increase. This compares two endpoints, rather than annual growth.

Six groups ended below their starting values

Abalone recorded the largest decrease: A$90.9 million. Gains elsewhere more than offset these declines. Price changes also affect nominal production value.

FROM GROWTH TO RETURN

Dollar changes reveal the drivers of growth.
Comparing value with volume reveals a different story.

Rock lobsters punch above their volume

Their implied production value was A$56.83 per kilogram. This measures production value, rather than a retail price.

Other finfish dominate quantity

Other finfish, sharks and rays supplied 48.3% of national production quantity but only 18.3% of value. Their large landed weight therefore contributed a much smaller share of the industry's production value.

Commonwealth fisheries reveal another side of the industry

Production value cannot tell us everything about wild fisheries. Understanding them also means examining what is caught, the condition of stocks and where vessels appear to fish.

Patterns across fisheries · 2002–2023
The heatmap compares annual catches; the bars follow your selection.

Catch trends diverged across fisheries

Commonwealth Trawl catch fell by about 49% between 2004 and 2023. Great Australian Bight Trawl also ended well below its starting level, while Small Pelagic catches rose strongly towards the end of its reported series. The fisheries did not follow one shared trend.

Catch volumes vary widely between fisheries

Annual catches exceeded 20,000 tonnes in Commonwealth Trawl and Small Pelagic, while Coral Sea catches stayed below 100 tonnes in every reported year. Pale cells can therefore represent consistently smaller fisheries, rather than missing activity. Grey cells indicate no reported data.

A larger catch does not establish healthier stocks

This ranking compares landed weight, rather than economic value. Stock assessments answer a different question: biological condition.

FROM CATCH TO CONDITION

Catch volumes alone do not reveal stock health.
Biological assessments show a different side of the story.

Five stocks moved out of overfished status

They were overfished in 2004, but classified not overfished in 2025. Examples include southern bluefin tuna and eastern orange roughy.

Eight stocks remained overfished

Eight stocks, including redfish and eastern gemfish, were classified overfished in 2004, 2014 and 2025. These repeated classifications show persistent low stock levels at the three checkpoints; they do not describe every intervening year.

FROM CONDITION TO ACTIVITY

Catch records measure the weight landed.
Vessel tracking adds another perspective: time spent fishing.
This view includes tracked vessels beyond Commonwealth-managed fisheries.

Most tracked activity lies in relatively few bins

In 2024, the busiest tenth of occupied hexagons contains 64% of estimated fishing hours in this extract. Tracked activity is concentrated in a small part of the mapped fishing footprint.

Tracking measures activity, rather than catch or health

Fishing hours are estimated from vessel movements. In 2024, the extract contains about 350,703 estimated fishing hours. Vessels without captured tracking signals are not represented. These estimates do not measure stock sustainability.
